My language skills

I've studied a few languages in my life, and, generally speaking, I have enjoyed learning about different cultures. Below, I have tried to evaluate my language skills in a bit of a rambling manner. Simultaneously, I'm practicing the use of tables in my web site.

Language Fluency Self-Evaluation
Finnish My first language, but it can always be improved upon. I wasn't particularly interested in Finnish when I was younger, but have later realized the value of being able to express yourself more coherently and colourfully.
English I consider myself quite fluent in English, but, as with Finnish, I'm always looking to improve. I have many friends and acquaintances abroad, and I get to practise my skills with them frequently. Much of the media I consume is in English, which helps immensely.
Swedish Akin to Finnish, I wasn't particularly interested in Swedish. I can appreciate the language and I don't particularly hate it, but it has merely fallen to disuse. I still recognize the basics, but wouldn't really be able to hold a conversation with a Swede.
French I began it maybe in 4th grade, but it slowly developed into a practice that I wasn't too fond of. I reluctantly pushed through with it until upper secondary school, but haven't used it since graduating. I might still remember the basics, but it has definitely degraded over the years.
Greek This is the most recent addition. Greek was initially quite peculiar, and I didn't really imagine speaking it one day, but fate brought me together with my spouse. I've been learning Greek, a small piece at a time, nearly every day for about 3 years. It is quite a challenging language, but I'm slowly building up the confidence, and the basics. Can't really pass as nothing but a tourist, despite my spouse's encouragement, but a day at a time.
